## Things I Learned

Today, I learned that AI systems can produce convincing but false information.

I also learned that hallucinations can appear in different forms, including fabricated facts, incorrect statistics, fake citations and incorrect attribution.

One important lesson was that additional information is not automatically a hallucination.

I also learned that an evaluator should assess individual factual claims rather than judge an entire response based on one correct or incorrect statement.

## Practical Exercise

I completed four hallucination detection exercises.

My practical assessment was 2/4 (50%).

The exercise exposed two important weaknesses:

1. Accepting an unsupported statistic without verifying its source.
2. Failing to identify an incorrect statement in a response that also contained correct information.

## Challenges

My biggest challenge was distinguishing between information that sounds plausible and information supported by evidence.

I also initially failed to recognise that an AI response can contain both accurate and inaccurate statements.

## What I Need to Improve

- Verify statistics before accepting them.
- Check the credibility of cited sources.
- Evaluate claims individually.
- Avoid accepting responses simply because they sound confident.
- Improve the quality of my evaluator explanations.
