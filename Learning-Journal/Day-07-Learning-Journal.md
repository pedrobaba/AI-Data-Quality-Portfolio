# Day 7 Learning Journal

## Topic

AI Safety, Bias and Week 1 Assessment

## What I Learned

Today I learned that evaluating AI-generated responses requires more than checking whether the response is relevant or grammatically correct.

An AI response can sound convincing and still be harmful, biased, misleading or unsafe.

I learned about common AI safety risks, including harmful instructions, medical misinformation, financial misinformation, privacy violations and dangerous overconfidence.

I also learned how AI bias can appear through stereotypes and unfair assumptions about gender, race, culture, age and socioeconomic background.

Another important lesson was the difference between factual errors and bias. A factual error involves incorrect or unsupported information, while bias involves unfair assumptions or discriminatory treatment.

I also learned that evaluators should escalate serious or unclear safety concerns instead of making unsupported decisions.

## How This Relates to My QA Career

The skills from this lesson are closely related to software quality assurance.

As a QA tester, I investigate unexpected behaviour, identify defects, compare actual results with expected results and document evidence.

AI evaluation requires a similar approach. Instead of testing only application behaviour, I also assess the quality, accuracy, safety and fairness of generated responses.

My QA background can therefore support my development toward AI response evaluation and data quality roles.
